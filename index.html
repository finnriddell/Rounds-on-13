a note for anyone who reads this page rather than watching it

What this is

Thirteen bells ringing every possible order of themselves: 6,227,020,800 rows, each rung exactly once. It began at 00:00 UTC on 15 November 2026 and comes back to its starting order around 28 June 2719. It runs on the clock, so everyone who opens it hears the same moment, and no one will ever witness it from start to finish.

Where it comes from

English change ringing, a tradition from the early 1600s. Ringers in church towers rang their bells in permutations, not tunes, under two rules: a bell may move only one place from one row to the next, and no row may ever be rung twice. Ringers call the second rule truth. They worked out the mathematics of permutations by ear and memory, a century before group theory gave it a name. This piece uses their oldest method, plain changes, stretched far past what any human band could ring.

What to look for

The piece contains itself. Take every 13th row, set aside the fastest bell, and what's left is this same piece on twelve bells. Take every 156th row and it's eleven bells, and so on down. Every bell is nudged whenever a quicker one crosses it, but set the quicker ones aside and each bell is keeping its own slower time: one step of the second bell takes thirteen rows, one step of the third takes 156, of the fourth 1,716, like a clock with thirteen hands. The ▸ lens lets you step down through those levels.

What you can't see from here

Almost all of it. On the day it was made, about 0.0002% had been rung. The slowest lenses will stay nearly empty for centuries.
